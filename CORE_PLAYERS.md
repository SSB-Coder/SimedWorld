# World: core players

Status: the active baseline funds 80 producer circles and 20 independent neural consumers. Detailed predator/anatomy/social designs below remain future or legacy experiments. Circle brains learn during life and transfer matching learned parameters at funded births; no team privileges, hidden planners or population reseeding are added.

## 1. The living participants

Use one underlying Organism model with different inherited capabilities. Producer, grazer, predator, scavenger, and decomposer describe how an organism currently obtains resources; they are not permanent software classes or assigned teams. A descendant can change feeding strategy as its anatomy, physiology, and controller evolve.

Expand the producer/consumer diagnostic founders as the corresponding functional capabilities become available:

| Founder population | Starting capabilities | Initial neural system | Main survival tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Producers | Capture light, absorb available mineral nutrients, construct tissue, reproduce locally | None required | Light-capture area and growth versus construction, maintenance, crowding, and being consumed |
| Mobile grazers | Sense, move, consume producers, grow, reproduce | Minimal ANN sized for the actual interface | Food access and threat avoidance versus movement, sensing, neural, and digestion costs |
| Pursuit-capable predators | Attack living prey with sustained movement and directional sensing | Independent inherited ANN | Capture success versus travel, attack, injury, and upkeep costs |
| Ambush-capable predators | Use positioning, local sensing, attack reach, and possible bursts | Independent inherited ANN | Detection and waiting versus missed prey and burst costs |
| Scavengers | Locate and ingest compatible carcass material | Independent ANN when active search is supported | Carcass access versus competition, search, and digestion costs |
| Recyclers | Process accessible dead organic matter, maintain themselves, grow, return unretained mineral nutrients | None required | Access to detritus and processing capacity versus maintenance, digestion costs, and competition |

These are starting configurations, not guaranteed outcomes. Producers initially use controlled traits, and recycling initially uses an aggregate process. Replace those scaffolds with evolving populations at the food-web milestone. Do not count scaffold behavior as evolved behavior.

Do not seed a guaranteed apex predator or designate a smartest organism. Living-tissue feeding, scavenging, mixed feeding, and defensive specialization become possible as the corresponding anatomy and rules are introduced. Whether any of these strategies persists is an experimental result.

### Producers

Producers supply the initial route from external light into usable chemical energy. Their growth also requires mineral nutrients; light alone cannot create tissue. They occupy space, store resources, incur maintenance costs, develop, reproduce, and die.

Candidate heritable traits include light-capture area, absorption capacity, storage, growth rate, offspring investment, and protective tissue. Spatial light variation and local resource competition provide opportunities for different strategies. Early producers have no foraging script or neural controller. Neural control or movement may evolve only when available modules and developmental mutations support them, with the same accounting as other organisms.

### Simple consumers

Consumers connect behavior to survival. Their anatomy supplies a local sensor interface, propulsion, ingestion, digestion, and storage. Their ANN requests actions; it does not receive a privileged command to find food or avoid a named predator.

The starting controller is small, not automatically a hundred-neuron network. Available observations can include resource cues, contact, energy reserves, and damage. Available actions include thrust, turning, feeding effort, and permitted growth/repair/reproduction allocation. The model determines which observations require sensor tissue and which bodily signals are available through explicit physiology.

Future feeding capabilities can include grazing, biting living tissue, scavenging, and mixed diets. Digestive efficiency depends on substrate and inherited investments. Supporting more food types has declared construction or maintenance tradeoffs. A consumer cannot ingest arbitrary matter simply because its analysis label becomes omnivore.

### Recyclers

Recyclers make decomposition a potential living strategy. They obtain chemical energy and material from dead organic substrates, retain some nutrients in their own tissue, release unretained mineral nutrients, dissipate energy through metabolism, and eventually die themselves.

Zero-neuron recyclers use explicit local uptake and processing physiology. They do not secretly navigate toward carcasses. Processing rate, substrate compatibility, maintenance, storage, and reproduction can evolve. Detailed enzymes, microbial chemistry, and neural recyclers are extensions rather than initial requirements.

While decomposition is an aggregate scaffold, it has an explicit substrate-consumption and mineral-release rate. During replacement by living recyclers, partition or disable that scaffold so both processes cannot consume the same material. No automatic release of all a carcass's nutrients while simultaneously awarding those nutrients to decomposer biomass.

### Social associations and communication

Groups consist of ordinary organisms with separate genomes, ANN weights, recurrent states, injuries, and reserves. Costed local signal emitters/receivers have bounded values, delay, range, attenuation, and lifetime. One brain cannot inspect another's private neural state or global group/target metadata. Reserve optional signal ports early; enable them after their interaction contract is validated.

Small organisms hunting larger prey must use actual contact, attack capability, paid effort, accumulated tissue damage, and prey defense. Concurrent attackers share one target inventory; subsequent carcass feeding or food sharing is accounted once. Recruitment, encirclement, defense, solitary behavior, and competition over kills are observed strategies. Initially infer group labels from interactions for display/analysis. Persistent recognition later requires observable cues and modeled memory rather than automatic team membership.

### Injury and repair

Record source, affected tissue/module, functional integrity, and repair progress. Damaged anatomy reduces corresponding sensing, movement, feeding, or protection; vital failure can kill. Repair spends nutrients, energy, and time. Health displays derive from tissue/function without creating another material pool. Show real injuries and recent attack events in the observer.

## 2. What every organism contains

| Component | Responsibility | Changes during life? | Heritable information |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and lineage | Stable individual ID, parent IDs, birth tick, ancestry | Events accumulate | Parent-child links are records, not behavioral instructions |
| Genome | Initial body, physiology, neural structure, learning/development rules | Fixed baseline in the initial model | Mutated copies fund offspring development |
| Body | Connected functional modules with geometry, tissue, sensor/actuator ports | Growth, damage, repair, later developmental changes | Construction and development instructions |
| Physiology | Energy/nutrient storage, maintenance, digestion, growth, maturity, repair | Continuously | Rates, capacities, efficiencies, and allocation rules |
| Optional brain | Action selection, recurrent state, later local learning | Activations/state; later weights and structure | Initial neural parameters/topology and permitted change rules |
| Expressed behavior | Actions and resulting interactions | Continuously | Wiring/development influences behavior; acquired state resets in offspring by default |

The genome is a blueprint, not a store of magically available organs. Offspring development, new tissue, neurons, and connections must be resource-funded. Stable module, port, neuron, and connection IDs preserve the meaning of surviving structures through mutations.

Basic maintenance and digestion are physiology shared by organisms with or without brains. A brain can influence declared allocation or action controls; it does not replace the physics or accounting rules. Basal threshold reproduction is available to zero-neuron organisms, while active organisms can later have explicitly costed reproductive control. Neither case receives a hidden survival planner.

Neural size does not assign an intelligence level. Support no neural controller, minimal controllers, and larger networks through hundreds or a few thousand neurons as profiling permits. Report input/output/hidden counts, synapses, neural state, and update rates rather than only a single neuron total.

## 3. Nonliving participants

| Participant | What it stores or supplies | How it enters the ecosystem | How it leaves that pool |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light field | External energy availability | Declared environmental input | Captured by producers or left uncaptured |
| Available mineral nutrients | Material needed for tissue | Initial finite budget; mineralization and excretion | Uptake into organisms; any explicit export |
| Living tissue | Embodied nutrients and chemical energy | Resource-funded growth and reproduction | Feeding, damage, metabolism, shedding, or death |
| Detritus | Dead organic matter, embodied nutrients, remaining chemical energy | Death, shed tissue, and organic waste | Consumption, decomposition, and any declared transport/export |
| Waste | Unassimilated or excreted resources | Digestion and metabolism | Direct mineral return or transfer to the appropriate detrital pool |
| Mechanical energy ledger | Tracked kinetic/potential energy when the physics model stores it | Funded actuator work or declared external mechanical input | Work/energy transfer and dissipation; never double-counted as immediate heat |
| Dissipated heat ledger | Energy no longer usable in this initial model | Maintenance, movement, digestion, neural activity, decomposition | Leaves the modeled usable-energy budget; never becomes free food |
| Habitat geometry | Boundaries, obstacles, and spatial opportunities | World configuration | Only explicit environmental changes or later terrain processes |

Start with a single limiting mineral nutrient and a small number of substrate categories. This is an ecological abstraction, not full chemistry. Living tissue is part of the organism's resource inventory, not a duplicate external food account. A carcass is dead matter; it does not retain a functioning ANN or reproduce.

Waste routing is mutually exclusive: mineral waste enters the available mineral pool, while organic waste retains its accounted chemical energy in detritus. Avoid storing the same resources simultaneously as waste, detritus, and free nutrients. Carcasses may initially be deposits in a field rather than one physics object per fragment; keep source metadata sufficient for trophic analysis where practical.

Do not respawn food from nothing. Resource abundance must follow light capture, nutrient availability, development, and actual transfers. For later dissolved-organic feeding or filtering, add an explicit organic resource pool rather than treating mineral nutrients as a chemical-energy source.

## 4. The shared interaction rules

| Interaction | Preconditions | Accounted outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capture light / absorb minerals | Appropriate tissue, local availability, capacity | Energy capture and nutrient transfer within production constraints |
| Sense | A declared sensor or bodily-signal interface | Local observations, with declared sensor/neural costs |
| Signal / receive | Emitter/receiver, local channel, funded operation | Bounded delayed environmental signal and costed observation; no access to another brain's private state |
| Move | Functional actuators and funded effort | Forces/motion, actuator costs, collisions |
| Attack / defend | Reach/contact, capable tissue, funded effort, explicit protection rules | Local damage/impairment and accounted debris; concurrent claims resolved once |
| Ingest and digest | Accessible substrate, compatible intake/digestion capability | Remove material once; allocate assimilation, waste, and dissipated energy |
| Grow or repair | Reserves, developmental capacity, existing damage where relevant | Transfer stored resources into tissue and pay construction costs |
| Reproduce | Maturity, viable development rules, offspring investment | Resource transfer from parent into a new individual, with recorded mutation and lineage |
| Die | Failure of vital tissue/maintenance under defined thresholds or destructive damage | Irreversible loss of organism activity and one transfer of remaining resources to dead-matter pools |
| Decompose | Accessible dead substrate and a valid recycler/scaffold | Substrate consumed, material partitioned, energy dissipated, mineral nutrients returned |

Resolve simultaneous claims on a shared resource using the documented tick rule. Two mouths cannot each receive the same bite. Contact or visible proximity alone does not transfer a resource. Feeding and reproduction are finite-rate processes, not unlimited operations inside one timestep.

For the initial lifecycle use juvenile -> mature -> dead, with reproduction as an event rather than a separate life stage. Define starvation, tissue damage, and maintenance failure concretely in the model contract. Do not invent an intelligence-dependent lifespan or require arbitrary old-age deaths to keep the population balanced. More detailed aging/repair mechanisms can be added explicitly later.

## 5. The experiment systems

These systems operate the experiment; they are not organisms competing inside it.

| System | Owner | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World and resource engine | Rust | Spatial fields, interactions, physiology, physics, budgets, biological clock |
| Genetic machinery | Rust | Offspring genomes, body/brain mutation, ancestry; no global champion tournament |
| Neural runtime | Python/PyTorch | Batched action selection, neural state, permitted learning; no privileged world knowledge |
| Experiment controller | Python | Seeds, declared scenarios, checkpoints, interventions, and evaluation runs |
| Observer | Bevy | Navigable world, scale-dependent detail, damage/signal inspection, and population/food-web summaries; no biological influence from rendering frequency |
| Archive and evaluator | Python with versioned exports | Preserve candidates and test copies separately; no ecological rewards from scores |

There is no separate evolution agent deciding that a population needs predators or smarter brains. Inheritance, variation, local interactions, survival, and reproduction generate changes in the population. Keep diagnostics, analysis labels, and test rankings out of neural observations unless represented by an explicit in-world signal.

## 6. First scenario and staged introduction

The first scenario has a light/nutrient grid, controlled producer organisms, a simple consumer population, and aggregate decomposition. Seed producer biomass and consumer reserves from the declared starting resource inventory rather than adding unrecorded material. Use a spatially heterogeneous habitat and modest population sizes chosen for inspection before scale benchmarks.

The extinction report, functional presets, module injury/energy records, camera navigation, and inherited ANN interfaces are implemented. Optional signal ports remain disabled. Introduce scalar variation before module-graph mutations and variable neural topology. Develop module damage/repair with anatomy, then expand compatible diets, living recyclers, and cooperative-hunting/signal experiments. Predator presets require real prey, contact, damage, and cost rules before use.

Observe local resource transfers, funded births, juvenile growth, deaths, nutrient return, ancestry, and phenotype changes. Stable coexistence is not an initial condition we enforce: report extinction and resource bottlenecks as well as persistent runs. Diagnostic controllers and scaffold populations remain explicitly labeled.

Before implementation, finalize the shared units, reserve/tissue compartments, vital thresholds, first module vocabulary, sensor/action schema, and the funded birth/death transaction. Those contracts apply to all living participants and form the first implementation milestone.
